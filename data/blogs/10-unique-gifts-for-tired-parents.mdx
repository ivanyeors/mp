Tired parents rarely need more things. What they want is a few hours of unbroken sleep, a darker room, or one evening without the dishes.

This guide lists ten gifts that help with those, each judged the same way. One is a pillow we make, and we say so in its entry. For choosing a pillow in general, see our [guide to choosing a pillow](/blog/how-to-choose-the-best-pillow-in-singapore).

Each gift had to give time back or improve the conditions for sleep. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) advises a quiet, dark bedroom at a cool, comfortable temperature. ## Which gifts give parents time back?

Babysitting, meals and cleaning all hand back hours, and none has to come from a shop.

### 1. A night of babysitting

- **Verdict:** Often the most useful gift for parents of young children, and it costs time rather than money.
- **Who it suits:** Parents of babies and toddlers without a helper or family nearby.
- **Pros:**
  - Gives a real block of rest, whether they sleep, nap or go out.
  - Can be repeated, for example one evening a month.
- **Cons:**
  - Needs trust. Offer a short daytime session first so everyone learns the routine.

### 2. A week of meals

- **Verdict:** Takes the daily "what's for dinner" job off their hands.
- **Who it suits:** New parents, and any family going through a hard week.
- **Pros:**
  - Home-cooked dishes and a meal delivery plan both work.
  - Portions can be frozen for later.
- **Cons:**
  - Check dietary needs and allergies first.
  - Small kitchens may lack freezer space.

### 3. A home cleaning session

- **Verdict:** Hands back a weekend morning that would have gone on chores.
- **Who it suits:** Working parents who spend weekends catching up on housework.
- **Pros:**
  - The result is visible straight away.
  - It adds nothing to a home already full of baby gear.
- **Cons:**
  - Some people dislike having strangers in their home.
  - It needs booking around nap times.

## Which gifts make the bedroom better for sleep?

Gifts that block light or noise, or keep the bed comfortable in the heat, make the bedroom better for sleep. Choose by the parent's main problem: light, noise, heat or night feeds.

### 4. Blackout curtains or blinds

- **Verdict:** The best answer to a bedroom lit by streetlights, a corridor light or early sun.
- **Who it suits:** Parents who nap in the day, shift workers, and bedrooms with bright windows.
- **Pros:**
  - Make daytime naps possible in a bright room.
  - Need no batteries or charging.
- **Cons:**
  - Need exact window measurements, so they are hard to give as a surprise.

### 5. A contoured sleep mask

- **Verdict:** Portable darkness for naps, travel and shared rooms.
- **Who it suits:** Parents who nap at odd hours, or share a room with a night-light.
- **Pros:**
  - Costs little and packs small.
  - Contoured cups keep pressure off the eyes.
- **Cons:**
  - Some people find masks warm in Singapore's heat.
  - Can slip off during the night.

### 6. Earplugs or a white-noise machine

- **Verdict:** Good for the parent who is off duty, not the one listening for the baby.
- **Who it suits:** Parents who take turns on night duty, and light sleepers near traffic or a snoring partner.
- **Pros:**
  - Muffle traffic, neighbours and snoring.
  - A white-noise machine masks sudden sounds without anything in the ears.
- **Cons:**
  - The parent on duty must still be able to hear the baby.
  - Steady noise can bother a partner who prefers silence.

### 7. A dimmable bedside light

- **Verdict:** Makes night feeds and nappy changes easier without switching on the main light.
- **Who it suits:** Parents of babies, and anyone who reads in bed while a partner sleeps.
- **Pros:**
  - A low, warm setting lights the task, not the whole room.
  - Less likely to wake a partner than the ceiling light.
- **Cons:**
  - Cables need to be tidy and out of reach of the cot.

### 8. Breathable cotton or linen sheets

- **Verdict:** A practical upgrade for hot, sticky nights.
- **Who it suits:** Hot sleepers, and homes where the air-conditioning goes off overnight.
- **Pros:**
  - Light, breathable weaves suit warm nights.
  - Cotton stands up to frequent hot washes.
- **Cons:**
  - You need the bed size, and colour is a personal choice.
  - Linen creases easily.

## Is a massage a good gift for tired parents?

Only if you also cover the childcare. Without it, the massage is one more appointment to arrange.

### 10. A massage, with childcare arranged

- **Verdict:** A reward that only works when someone else has the children.
- **Who it suits:** Parents who rarely get an hour to themselves.
- **Pros:**
  - An hour where nobody needs them.
  - Can be booked near home, or at home.
- **Cons:**
  - Only useful with childcare covered, so pair it with gift 1.
  - Anyone pregnant or recently postnatal should check with their doctor first.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good gift for exhausted new parents?

Time is usually the most welcome gift: a night of babysitting, a week of meals or a cleaning session. Bedroom gifts such as blackout curtains help too, if they suit the parents' room.

### Can you give a custom-fitted pillow as a surprise?

Not easily, because the pillow is sized from the recipient's own measurements. Tell them about the gift, then let them measure at home or book a studio measurement.

### Can someone who received an Mpillow as a gift book a re-adjustment?

Yes. If the pillow is not listed in their account because it was a gift, they can still book a re-adjustment and bring proof of purchase.

### What should you avoid giving parents of a baby?

Avoid pillows, bolsters, cot bumpers and soft toys for the baby's cot. The American Academy of Pediatrics advises keeping soft objects out of a baby's sleep area for the first 12 months. [[cite: AAP 2022: babies should sleep in a crib or bassinet, on their back, without soft toys, pillows, blankets or other bedding (first year) | https://www.aap.org/en/news-room/news-releases/aap/2022/american-academy-of-pediatrics-updates-safe-sleep-recommendations-back-is-best]] Our page on [pillows for kids](/blog/how-adjustable-pillow-firmness-can-help-kids-sleep-better-the-key-to-restful-nights) covers when a child can start using one.

### Should both parents wear earplugs at night?

No. One parent should always be able to hear the baby, so earplugs suit whoever is off duty that night.
