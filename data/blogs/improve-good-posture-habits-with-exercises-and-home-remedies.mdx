Before you read on, notice how you are sitting. Is your head drifting towards the screen, or is your back curving into the chair? Most of us slip into these positions without noticing, and by evening the neck and shoulders feel it.

This page covers practical posture habits, six simple exercises, how posture affects your breathing, and when to get help. It is part of our guide to the [best pillow for neck pain](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore), which covers your sleep setup.

## Is there one correct posture?

No. A physiotherapy leaflet from [South Tees Hospitals NHS](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Posture-Reformed-A5-4pp-V2.pdf) says there is no single "correct" posture. Natural differences in the curve of the spine are normal. Moving and changing position regularly can help.

That said, long periods with your head pushed forward over a phone are linked with neck pain, shoulder tightness and headaches ([SingHealth HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/how-to-prevent/neck-pain/text-neck-syndrome-symptoms-prevention)). The aim is a position you can relax in, and plenty of movement between positions.

## What daily habits improve posture?

Set up your desk so you do not have to reach or look down, and get up regularly. The same checks apply to your phone, your laptop and reading in bed.

### At your desk

- **Sit back in the chair** with your bottom at the back of the seat and your lower back supported ([South Tees Hospitals NHS](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/posture-2/)).
- **Keep your feet flat** on the floor, with your knees level with your hips, as South Tees also advises. If your feet do not reach, [Singapore General Hospital](https://www.sgh.com.sg/patient-care/conditions-treatments/office-ergonomics-occupational-overuse) suggests a footrest so they are not left dangling.
- **Rest your forearms** with your elbows bent to about a right angle and the desk just below elbow height, as SGH advises. Keep your upper arms close to your body ([Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/office-ergonomics/art-20046169)).
- **Raise your screen** so the top sits at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away, as Mayo Clinic recommends.
- **Get up about every 30 minutes**, even for a minute. SGH says some form of break from sitting every 30 minutes is beneficial.

### On your phone and laptop

- **Bring the screen up** rather than bending your neck to look down at it. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581) advises holding small screens straight out instead. Our page on [what tech neck is and how to ease it](/blog/what-is-tech-neck-and-how-can-a-pillow-help-fix-it-in-singapore) goes further.
- **Give a laptop a stand** and use a separate keyboard and mouse, so the screen can sit higher. Mayo Clinic suggests this set-up for a laptop used at a desk.

| Setting | Quick check | Quick change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office desk | Is the top of the screen near eye level? | Raise the monitor on a stand |
| Laptop | Are you looking down at the screen? | Use a stand and a separate keyboard |
| Phone | Is your chin close to your chest? | Bring the phone up towards eye level |
| Long meetings or calls | Have you been still for 30 minutes? | Stand, walk or stretch for a minute |
| Reading in bed | Is your head propped on stacked pillows? | Sit up with support behind your back |

## Which exercises help posture?

Simple mobility and strengthening exercises for the neck, shoulders and upper back keep the muscles that hold you upright working. For long-lasting neck pain, a [2015 Cochrane review](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004250.pub5/full) found moderate-quality evidence that strength training for the neck, shoulders and upper back eases pain.

Move slowly and stay within comfort. A mild, dull ache is normal at first, but severe pain means you have gone too far ([North Cheshire and Mersey NHS](https://northcheshireandmersey.nhs.uk/media/emlfdohl/neck-range-of-motion-exercises-patient-advice-sheet.pdf)). Stop if an exercise makes your symptoms worse or causes new pain ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/neck-and-back-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-neck-problems)). If you have a diagnosed neck or back condition, ask a physiotherapist for a programme made for you.

Most of these can be done at your desk or during a short break. The doorway stretch needs a door frame, and the last one needs a mat, a rug or your bed.

### Chin tuck

Sit up straight and look at a point straight ahead. Draw your chin in towards your neck, as if making a double chin. Hold for about three seconds, relax, and repeat 10 times ([Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS](https://flipbooks.leedsth.nhs.uk/LN002635.pdf)).

### Neck turns and tilts

Sit up straight and slowly turn your head to one side, as far as is comfortable. Hold for about three seconds, then turn to the other side, as the Leeds leaflet describes. Next, tilt your head towards one shoulder until you feel a stretch on the opposite side. Hold for 5 to 10 seconds, then repeat on the other side ([NHS Fife](https://www.nhsfife.org/media/2sfgwql9/neck_pain_v7.pdf)).

### Shoulder rolls

Sitting or standing, roll your shoulders slowly in a circle, then reverse the direction. NHS Fife's neck pain leaflet suggests 5 to 10 times in each direction.

### Shoulder blade squeeze

Sit tall and gently draw your shoulder blades back and down. Hold for up to 10 seconds, then relax ([Cambridge University Hospitals](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/scapular-stabilisation-exercises/)).

### Doorway chest stretch

Stand in a doorway with your forearm against the frame and your elbow at shoulder height. Ease your body forward until you feel a gentle stretch across the front of your chest. Hold, then swap sides ([South Tees Hospitals NHS](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/anterior-shoulder-stretch/)).

### Face-down shoulder blade lift

Lie face down with your forehead on the floor and your elbows bent at right angles. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and lift your elbows and forearms off the floor. Hold briefly, then lower ([Cambridge University Hospitals](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/scapula-position/)).

## How does posture affect your breathing?

Slumping, or pushing your head forward, leaves your chest less room to expand. In a [2019 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6348172/) of 15 healthy men, lung-function readings were lower with the head pushed forward than in a neutral position. In a [2016 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4905897/), people who spent an hour seated using a smartphone had lower readings than a group who did not. Both studies were small.

You can feel the difference yourself. Sit slumped with your head forward and take a slow, deep breath. Then sit tall, let your shoulders drop, and breathe in again. Notice whether the second breath feels different.

The same idea applies at night. A pillow that is too high or stiff keeps your neck bent forward overnight, much as slouching does by day. [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/say-good-night-to-neck-pain) links this with morning pain and stiffness.

Posture is not the answer to every breathing problem at night. Breathing that stops and starts, gasping or choking noises, or feeling very tired during the day can be signs of sleep apnoea. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/) advises seeing a GP if you have these symptoms. ## Does your sleep position affect your posture?

Your sleep position decides how your neck and back are supported for hours, though it will not change how you stand during the day. Harvard Health names side and back sleeping as the two positions easiest on the neck.

- **On your side:** use a pillow high enough for your shoulder width. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852) says bending your knees with a pillow between them can help line up your spine, pelvis and hips.
- **On your back:** use a lower pillow with support under your neck, and try a pillow under your knees. Mayo Clinic says this may help relax your back muscles.
- **On your front:** your neck stays turned to one side. [HealthHub Singapore](https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain) advises people with neck pain to try not to sleep on their stomach.

## When should you see a doctor or physiotherapist?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if posture-related neck or back pain has not improved after a few weeks of self-help, or keeps coming back. For neck pain, the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/neck-pain-and-stiff-neck/) advises seeing a GP if pain or stiffness does not go away after a few weeks.

Get help sooner if the pain spreads into your arms or legs, or comes with numbness, tingling or weakness. The same applies to pain that follows a fall or injury. Mayo Clinic lists each of these as a reason to get neck pain checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best sitting position for long hours?

There is no single best position. Sit back with your lower back supported and your screen near eye level, and change position or stand up about every 30 minutes.

### How often should I do posture exercises?

A few repetitions of each exercise, several times a day, is a practical starting point. Desk breaks are an easy time to fit them in.

### Should posture exercises hurt?

No. NHS physiotherapy advice says a mild, dull ache is normal at first, but severe pain means you have gone too far. Stop if an exercise makes your symptoms worse or causes new pain. If you have a diagnosed neck or back condition, ask a physiotherapist for a programme made for you.

### Does slouching affect breathing?

Small studies suggest it can. In one, healthy men had lower lung-function readings with the head pushed forward than in a neutral position. Sitting tall gives your chest more room to expand.

### Can a pillow improve my posture?

A pillow cannot change how you sit or stand during the day. The right height keeps your neck in line with your spine while you sleep, so the night does not add to the strain.
