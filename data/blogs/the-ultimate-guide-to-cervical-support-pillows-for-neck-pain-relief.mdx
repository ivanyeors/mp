If your neck feels stiff every morning, a pillow shaped like a wave can look like the obvious answer. Cervical pillows do suit some people well, but the shape is only part of what makes a pillow work.

This guide explains what a cervical pillow is, what the research says, who it suits, and how to choose its height. It is part of our in-depth guide to the [best pillow for neck pain](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore).

## What is a cervical pillow?

A cervical pillow is designed to support your neck, not only your head. "Cervical" refers to the seven vertebrae of the neck, which form a gentle inward curve when seen from the side. [[cite: cervical spine has seven vertebrae (C1-C7) with a natural lordotic (inward) curve | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539734/]]

Cervical pillows come in a few common designs:

- **Contour or wave foam:** a moulded block with a raised roll under the neck and a dip for the head. Many have a higher and a lower edge.
- **Neck roll:** a firm cylinder that sits under the neck, used alone or tucked inside a softer pillow.
- **Adjustable fill:** a pillow you can open to add or remove filling until the neck area is supported.
- **Zoned designs:** pillows with different heights for back and side sleeping, such as the M-shaped Mpillow.

## How is a cervical pillow meant to support the neck?

A cervical pillow fills the space under your neck, so your muscles do not have to hold your head in position all night. On your back, the neck rests on the roll while your head sits slightly lower.

Harvard Health describes this set-up for back sleepers: a rounded support under the neck, with a flatter pillow for the head. It also warns that too much height behind the neck pushes the head forward. [[cite: Harvard Health: back sleepers - rounded neck support with a flatter head pillow; too large a pillow pushes the head forward | https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/say-good-night-to-neck-pain]]

The height of that roll matters more than the material. A 2021 review found that a pillow's shape and height affected neck alignment more than what it was made of. [[cite: Pang, Tsang & Fu 2021: cervical alignment affected more by pillow shape and height than by material | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/]]

## Does a cervical pillow actually help neck pain?

A cervical pillow can help some people, but the evidence for the contour shape on its own is weak. How well the pillow fits your body and sleep position matters more than what it is called.

What the research shows:

- **Pillows in general:** a 2021 review found that pillows reduced waking pain and neck disability in people with chronic neck pain. They did not improve sleep quality. [[cite: Pang, Tsang & Fu 2021: pillow use reduced waking pain and neck disability; no effect on sleep quality | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/]]
- **Contour versus regular:** in a study of 106 side sleepers, a foam contour pillow did no better than a regular foam pillow. A latex pillow did best. [[cite: Gordon, Grimmer-Somers & Trott 2009, Manual Therapy: foam contour no advantage over regular foam; rubber pillow best | https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/11358/]]
- **Clinical advice in Singapore:** a Sengkang General Hospital physiotherapist says there is no strong evidence that specific cervical pillows reduce or prevent neck pain. The best pillow is one that feels comfortable and suits how you sleep. [[cite: SKH physiotherapist: no strong evidence specific cervical pillows reduce or prevent neck pain; choose a comfortable pillow that fits your sleep position | https://www.skh.com.sg/about-skh/news/skoop/skoop-issue-16/not-all-it-s-cracked-up-to-be]]

**What a cervical pillow can do:** support the curve of your neck and hold your head at a steady height. It also removes the strain of a pillow that was too high or too flat.

**What it cannot do:** change how you sit and stand during the day, undo an injury, or explain pain that persists. ## Who does a cervical pillow suit, and who should skip it?

A cervical pillow suits people who sleep mostly on their back or side and like firm, defined support. It suits stomach sleepers and restless sleepers less well.

| Sleeper | Does a cervical pillow suit? | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back sleeper | Often | A neck roll that fills the curve without tipping the chin up |
| Side sleeper | Yes, if the height matches the shoulders | Enough height to keep the head level |
| Side and back | Only with two heights | Higher side areas and a lower centre |
| Stomach sleeper | Rarely | A very low pillow, and a gradual move to side sleeping |
| Moves a lot at night | Sometimes | Adjustable fill rather than a rigid foam contour |
| Flatter neck curve | Sometimes | A gentle roll, since a high one can feel like a bump |

Stomach sleeping is the main poor fit. It keeps your head turned for hours, and HealthHub Singapore advises people with neck pain to avoid it. [[cite: HealthHub Singapore neck pain: avoid sleeping on your stomach | https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain]]

If you have had neck surgery or have a diagnosed neck condition, ask your surgeon or physiotherapist before you change pillows.

## How do you choose the right cervical pillow height?

Choose the height that keeps your head level with your spine in the position you sleep in most. Research has not settled on one ideal height, so the answer depends on your build. [[cite: Lei et al. 2021 review: optimal pillow height for supine and lateral positions cannot yet be identified | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8544534/]]

1. **Know your main position.** Notice how you fall asleep and how you wake. If you use both side and back, you need two heights.
2. **Side sleepers: start from your shoulders.** Broader shoulders need a higher pillow. Mpillow sets pillow height from your shoulder length.
3. **Back sleepers: check your chin.** Your face should point straight up, with your chin neither tucked in nor tipped back.
4. **Test it lying down.** Spend a few minutes in your usual position, and ask someone to photograph you from the front or side.
5. **Prefer adjustable.** A pillow you can add filling to, or take filling from, can change with you and with your mattress.

Signs the height is wrong:

- **Too high:** on your side, your head tilts up towards the ceiling. On your back, your chin is pushed towards your chest.
- **Too low:** on your side, your head drops towards the mattress. On your back, your head tips back and the roll does not reach your neck.

## When should you see a doctor instead of changing pillows?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if neck pain lasts more than a few weeks or keeps getting worse. The same applies if it comes with numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands. Get urgent help if the pain started after an accident or fall. [[cite: NHS neck pain: when to see a GP and when to get urgent help | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neck-pain/]]

A different pillow will not help pain that comes from an injury or a condition in the neck itself. Our neck pain guide lists [the warning signs in full](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore#when-should-you-see-a-doctor-about-neck-or-shoulder-pain).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are cervical pillows good for side sleepers?

They can be, if the height matches your shoulder width so your head stays level. A contour that is too low for broad shoulders lets your head drop towards the mattress.

### Can a cervical pillow make neck pain worse?

It can if the roll is too high or too firm for your neck, or if you sleep on your stomach. If your pain increases after switching, go back to your old pillow, and get advice if the pain continues.

### Is a cervical pillow good for stomach sleepers?

Usually not. Stomach sleeping keeps the neck turned for hours, and a raised neck roll adds to that bend.

### Is a memory foam contour pillow the same as a cervical pillow?

It is one type. Cervical support can also come from a neck roll, adjustable filling, or a zoned design such as the Mpillow.
