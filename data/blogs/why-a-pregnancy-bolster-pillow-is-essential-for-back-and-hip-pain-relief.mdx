As your bump grows, the positions you used to sleep in stop working. Lying on your back feels heavy, your hips ache on your side, and you may wake several times a night to turn over. This page explains why side sleeping is advised in later pregnancy and how to position a bolster for your back and hips. It also covers what to look for in one. For choosing a bolster in general, see our [guide to choosing a bolster](/blog/the-complete-guide-to-choosing-the-right-bolster-in-singapore).

This is general information. Your midwife or doctor knows your pregnancy, so follow their advice first.

## Why is side sleeping advised in later pregnancy?

From the third trimester, health services advise going to sleep on your side. The NHS says that after 28 weeks, going to sleep on your back is linked to a higher risk of stillbirth. It says the safest position is on your side. [[cite: NHS: after 28 weeks, going to sleep on your back may double the risk of stillbirth; the safest position is on either side | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/related-conditions/common-symptoms/tiredness/]]

ACOG, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, explains why. In the second and third trimesters, lying on your back may press on a major blood vessel that carries blood to the uterus. [[cite: ACOG: in the second and third trimesters, lying on the back may compress a major blood vessel that takes blood to the uterus; side sleeping may be best | https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/ask-acog/can-i-sleep-on-my-back-when-im-pregnant]]

### What if you wake up on your back?

Do not panic. The advice is about the position you go to sleep in. If you wake on your back, turn onto your side and go back to sleep. [[cite: If you wake on your back, turn onto your side and go back to sleep | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/related-conditions/common-symptoms/tiredness/]] [[cite: ACOG: if you wake on your back, don't worry; roll onto one side | https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/the-latest/the-top-6-pregnancy-questions-i-hear-from-first-time-moms]]

### Left side or right side?

You may be told the left side is better. ACOG's guidance is that either side is fine. [[cite: ACOG: you can sleep on either side, left or right | https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/the-latest/the-top-6-pregnancy-questions-i-hear-from-first-time-moms]] Some advice, including Singapore's HealthHub, favours the left side. [[cite: HealthHub Singapore: pregnant women are generally advised to sleep on their left side to improve circulation to the foetus | https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/personal-care/8-sleeping-positions-to-improve-your-health]] If your midwife or doctor has given you specific advice, follow it.

## How do you position a bolster for back and hip comfort?

Lie on your side with your knees bent and a bolster between your knees, so your top leg rests level with your hip. Add a small cushion under your bump, and something behind your back if you tend to roll. ACOG suggests the same set-up: a pillow between the knees and one under the belly, or a full-length body pillow. [[cite: ACOG: side sleeping with knees bent, a pillow between the knees and under the belly, or a full-length body pillow | https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/ask-acog/can-i-sleep-on-my-back-when-im-pregnant]]

Step by step:

1. **Lie on your side with your knees bent.** Bending the knees slightly takes some pressure off the spine. [[cite: Flexing the knees with a pillow between the legs aligns spine, pelvis and hips and takes pressure off the spine | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]]
2. **Place the bolster between your knees,** and down to your ankles if it is long enough. This stops your top leg sliding forward and twisting your pelvis.
3. **Support your bump.** A small pillow or folded towel under the bump can take some of its weight.
4. **Hug the top end of the bolster,** or a pillow in front of you, so your top arm and shoulder have somewhere to rest.
5. **Put a pillow behind your back** if you keep rolling over. It gives you something to lean into and keeps you on your side.
6. **Keep your head on its own pillow,** high enough to keep your neck level with your spine.

### Turning over in bed

Turning over can be the most uncomfortable part of the night, especially with pelvic pain. Keep your knees together as you roll, and bring the bolster with you. [[cite: Pelvic pain in pregnancy: keep knees together when turning in bed; sleep with a pillow between the legs | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pelvic-pain/]]

## Can a bolster help with pelvic girdle pain?

The NHS lists sleeping with a pillow between your legs as one self-help step for pelvic pain in pregnancy. [[cite: NHS self-help for pregnancy pelvic pain includes sleeping with a pillow between the legs | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pelvic-pain/]] A bolster can do that job, and may make lying on your side more comfortable.

A bolster does not address the cause of the pain. If you have pain in your pelvis, tell your midwife or doctor. They can refer you to a physiotherapist who works with pregnancy-related pain. [[cite: Tell your midwife or GP about pelvic pain; they can refer you to a physiotherapist | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pelvic-pain/]]

## What should you look for in a pregnancy bolster?

Look for a bolster that reaches from your chest to your knees and will not flatten between them. It should also be easy to wash properly. You do not need a product labelled for pregnancy. Ordinary pillows, a bolster or a body pillow can all be used to support side sleeping.

| Option | What it supports | Bed space | Washing | After pregnancy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular pillows | Knees, bump or back, one at a time | Small, but they drift apart overnight | Usually easy | Back to everyday use |
| Straight bolster | Knees and top arm, together | Moderate | Depends on the build | Side sleeping |
| Long body pillow | Front, knees and ankles | Large | Often bulky to wash whole | Side sleeping, if you have space |
| C- or U-shaped pregnancy pillow | Front, back, knees and bump at once | Very large | Often too bulky for a home machine | Bulky to keep once you no longer need it |

Four things to check:

- **Length for your height.** The bolster should reach between your knees while you hug its top end.
- **Firmness you can change.** Your body changes month by month, so a bolster you can soften or firm up stays useful for longer.
- **Washability.** In Singapore's humid climate, you will want to wash the whole bolster, not just its case.
- **Space.** In a shared bed, a straight bolster leaves more room than a U-shaped pillow.

For a closer look at the differences, see our comparison of [bolsters, body pillows and pillows](/blog/bolsters-vs-pillows-do-you-really-need-both).

## When should you call your midwife or doctor about back or hip pain?

Back pain is common in pregnancy, but some pain needs checking. [[cite: Back pain is common during pregnancy | https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/back-pain-during-pregnancy]] Contact your midwife or doctor urgently if back pain comes with any of these:

- a fever
- bleeding from the vagina
- pain when you pee
- pain under your ribs

[[cite: NHS: contact GP or midwife urgently if back pain comes with fever, vaginal bleeding, pain when peeing, or pain under the ribs | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/back-pain/]]

Go to A&E immediately if back pain comes with loss of feeling in one or both legs, your bottom or your genitals. [[cite: NHS: emergency care if back pain comes with loss of feeling in legs, bottom or genitals | https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/back-pain/]]

Also tell your midwife or doctor if back or hip pain:

- is severe, or keeps getting worse
- does not settle with rest and changes of position
- stops you sleeping, walking or getting through your day

## Frequently asked questions

### Which side should I sleep on when I am pregnant?

ACOG says either side is fine. Some guidance favours the left side, so ask your midwife or doctor if you have been given specific advice for your pregnancy.

### What should I do if I wake up on my back?

Turn onto your side and go back to sleep. The NHS advice is about the position you go to sleep in, from 28 weeks onwards.

### When should I start using a bolster in pregnancy?

Start whenever it makes you more comfortable. The NHS advises going to sleep on your side from 28 weeks, so it helps to have your support ready before then.

### Can I use an ordinary bolster instead of a pregnancy pillow?

Yes. You do not need a product labelled for pregnancy. A bolster between your knees, with a small cushion under your bump, supports side sleeping in the same way.

### Can I keep using my bolster after the baby arrives?

Yes, for your own side sleeping. Never put a bolster or pillow in your baby's cot. For the first year, a baby's sleep space should hold only a firm, flat mattress and fitted sheet. [[cite: AAP: for the first year, nothing in the crib but a firm mattress and fitted sheet | https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/a-parents-guide-to-safe-sleep.aspx]]
