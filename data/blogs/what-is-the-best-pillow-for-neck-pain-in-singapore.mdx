Waking up with a neck that will not turn, or a shoulder that aches before the alarm goes off, makes for a long day. It is natural to blame the pillow, and sometimes the pillow really is part of the problem.

This guide explains what a pillow can and cannot do for neck and shoulder pain. It covers height for each sleep position and body build, firmness, shape and fillings, how to tell when the pillow is not the cause, and the warning signs that mean seeing a doctor.

## Can the wrong pillow cause neck pain?

Yes, it can contribute. A pillow that is too high, too flat or worn out can hold your neck bent for hours, and that strain often shows up as stiffness in the morning.

Mayo Clinic lists sleeping in an awkward position, or with too many or too few pillows, among the common causes of neck pain. [[cite: Mayo Clinic lists sleeping position and too many or too few pillows among causes of neck pain | https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/neck-pain/basics/causes/sym-20050882]]

Pillows do make a measurable difference for some people. A 2021 review of pillow studies found that pillows reduced waking neck pain and neck disability in people with neck pain, although they did not change overall sleep quality. [[cite: Pang, Tsang & Fu 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis: pillow use reduced waking pain and neck disability; no effect on sleep quality | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/]]

Sleep posture seems to matter too. In a small study that filmed people asleep, those who woke with neck symptoms spent more of the night in poorly supported positions than people without symptoms. [[cite: Cary, Jacques & Briffa 2021 (check the 2024 correction, PMC11218933): cervical-symptom group spent more time in provocative sleep postures | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631621/]]

A pillow is still only one factor. It cannot undo a day hunched over a laptop, an old injury or wear in the neck joints. Those causes are covered further down this page.

## What should a pillow do for your neck?

A good pillow keeps your head, neck and upper back in roughly the line they hold when you stand tall. Your neck muscles can then rest instead of holding your head in place all night.

Mayo Clinic's advice is to keep your head and neck aligned with your body, with a small pillow under the neck. [[cite: Mayo Clinic neck pain prevention: head and neck aligned with body, small pillow under the neck | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581]]

You can check your own alignment. Lie in your usual position and ask someone to take a photo:

- **On your side:** your nose should line up with the middle of your chest. Your head should not tilt up towards the ceiling or down towards the mattress.
- **On your back:** your face should point straight up. Your chin should be neither pushed towards your chest nor tipped back.
- **Either way:** the pillow should fill the space under your neck, not only sit under your head.

## How high should a pillow be for your sleep position?

Pillow height depends on how you sleep and how you are built, so there is no single correct number. A 2021 review of pillow-height research concluded that an ideal height for back or side sleeping cannot yet be set, because the evidence is too thin. [[cite: Lei et al. 2021 review: optimal pillow height for supine and lateral positions cannot yet be identified | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8544534/]]

What can be said is what the pillow has to do in each position.

| Sleep position | What the pillow must do | Height | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Side | Fill the gap between ear and mattress so the head stays level | Highest; rises with shoulder width | A thin pillow that lets the head drop |
| Back | Support the curve of the neck without pushing the head forward | Lower than for side sleeping | A thick pillow that bends the chin towards the chest |
| Stomach | Keep the turned head as flat as possible | As low as possible | Expecting a better pillow to make this position comfortable |
| Side and back | Give two heights in one pillow | Higher at the sides, lower in the centre | One even height that suits neither position |

### Side sleepers: let your shoulders set the height

Side sleeping needs the most height, because your shoulder lifts your head off the mattress. Broad shoulders leave a bigger gap to fill than narrow ones.

Harvard Health advises side sleepers to use a pillow that is higher under the neck than under the head, which keeps the spine straight. [[cite: Harvard Health: side sleepers need a pillow higher under the neck than under the head to keep the spine straight | https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/say-good-night-to-neck-pain]]

Mpillow sets pillow height from your shoulder length for the same reason. Our [side sleeper pillow guide](/blog/side-sleeper-pillow-your-ultimate-guide-for-alignment-pain-relief) covers knee support and combination sleeping in more detail.

### Back sleepers: lower, with support under the neck

Back sleeping needs less height. The aim is to support the inward curve of the neck while the back of the head rests a little lower.

Harvard Health suggests a rounded support under the neck with a flatter area for the head. It also warns that a pillow that is too big behind the neck pushes the head forward. [[cite: Harvard Health: back sleepers - rounded neck support with flatter head pillow; too large a pillow pushes the head forward | https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/say-good-night-to-neck-pain]]

Some back sleepers also find a pillow under the knees helps. Mayo Clinic suggests it to help keep the lower back's natural curve. [[cite: Mayo Clinic sleeping positions for back pain: pillow under the knees for back sleepers | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]]

### Stomach sleepers: the hardest position for the neck

Sleeping on your front keeps your head turned to one side for hours, and no pillow fully solves that. HealthHub Singapore advises people with neck pain to avoid sleeping on their stomach. [[cite: HealthHub Singapore neck pain: avoid sleeping on your stomach | https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain]]

Mpillow recommends side or back sleeping for the same reason, and suggests stomach sleepers try moving to their side. A bolster can make the change easier. Our guide to [using a bolster for side sleeping](/blog/how-to-use-a-bolster-pillow-for-better-side-sleeping-in-singapore-homes) shows how.

### Combination sleepers: two heights in one pillow

If you fall asleep on your back and wake on your side, one even height is wrong for half the night. You need a pillow that is higher where your head lands on your side, and lower in the middle.

## Should a pillow for neck pain be firm or soft?

Choose a pillow firm enough to hold its height all night and soft enough to be comfortable. Too soft and your head sinks; too firm or too full and your neck stays bent.

Research on pillow materials is limited, but one study is useful here. Among 106 side sleepers, a latex (rubber) pillow came out best for waking neck pain and comfort, while feather pillows came out worst. [[cite: Gordon, Grimmer-Somers & Trott 2009, Manual Therapy: side sleepers; rubber pillow best for waking cervical pain and comfort, feather pillows not recommended | https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/11358/]]

The 2021 review mentioned above also suggested that a pillow's shape and height affect neck alignment more than its material. [[cite: Pang, Tsang & Fu 2021: cervical alignment affected more by pillow shape and height than by material | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/]]

Firmness is also personal, which is why adjustable filling helps. The Basic Mpillow is the softer of the two tiers. The Premium Mpillow's firmness is customised with a mix of softer hollow-fibre pouches and firmer fibre-ball pouches.

## Is a contour or cervical pillow better than a regular one?

Not automatically. A contour pillow can suit back and side sleepers who like a defined roll under the neck, but the shape alone has not been shown to beat a well-fitted regular pillow.

In the same side-sleeper study, the foam contour pillow did no better than a regular foam pillow. [[cite: Gordon et al. 2009: foam contour pillow showed no advantage over a regular-shaped foam pillow | https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/11358/]] Height and fit matter more than the shape on the label.

| Pillow shape | Suits | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular (rectangular) | Most sleepers, if the height is right | Flattening over time; one height for every position |
| Contour or cervical (wave) | Back sleepers and steady side sleepers | A fixed roll height; can feel hard if you move a lot |
| Adjustable fill | Anyone who needs to tune the height | Takes some trial and error to set up |
| Zoned, like the M-shaped Mpillow | People who switch between side and back | Needs the right size and its own case |

## How can you stop shoulder pain when you sleep?

Start by keeping off the sore shoulder, then make sure your pillow is high enough for side sleeping. Position is the first thing to change, before buying anything.

People with pain in one shoulder often sleep on that side. In a Danish study of 83 patients, two in three people who slept on one side lay on their painful shoulder, though the study could not show which came first. [[cite: Kempf & Kongsted 2012, J Manipulative Physiol Ther: 67% of one-side sleepers lay on the painful shoulder; causality unknown | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22608285/]]

Pillow choice may play a part too. A study of side sleepers found that the type of pillow made a difference to shoulder-blade and arm pain on waking. [[cite: Gordon, Grimmer-Somers & Trott 2010, J Pain Res: pillow type and waking scapular/arm pain in side sleepers | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3004642/]]

Things to try tonight:

- **Sleep on the other side or your back.** Public health advice for shoulder pain suggests avoiding lying on the painful side. [[cite: HSE Ireland shoulder pain: avoid sleeping on the painful side | https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/shoulder-pain/]]
- **Stop yourself rolling over.** A pillow placed along your back makes it harder to roll onto the sore side in your sleep. [[cite: NHS trust shoulder pain leaflet: place a pillow along your back to stop rolling onto the painful side | https://www.leicspart.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LPT-CHSMSK12-Shoulder-Pain.pdf]]
- **Check the pillow height.** If your head drops towards the mattress when you lie on your side, the pillow is too low for your shoulder width.
- **Use the bolster you already have.** Many Singapore homes sleep with a bolster. Mpillow supports the neck and the Mbolster supports the torso for side sleeping; see our [bolster buying guide](/blog/the-complete-guide-to-choosing-the-right-bolster-in-singapore).

See a GP if shoulder pain is not improving after two weeks, or if moving your arm or shoulder is very difficult. Get urgent help for sudden or severe pain, an arm you cannot move, a change in the shoulder's shape, numbness that does not go away, or pain after a fall or injury. [[cite: NHS shoulder pain: see a GP if not improved after 2 weeks; urgent signs listed | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shoulder-pain/]]

## When is your pillow not the cause of neck pain?

When the pain builds during the day, follows an injury or comes with other symptoms, look beyond the pillow. A new pillow will not help a problem that starts somewhere else.

Mayo Clinic lists poor posture, muscle strain, worn joints, nerve compression and injuries such as whiplash among the common causes of neck pain. [[cite: Mayo Clinic neck pain causes: poor posture, muscle strain, osteoarthritis and cervical spondylosis, nerve compression, whiplash and other injuries | https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/neck-pain/basics/causes/sym-20050882]] A pillow can reduce strain at night, but it does not address any of these.

- **Desk and phone posture.** Long hours looking down at a screen load the neck. Read [what tech neck is and what helps](/blog/what-is-tech-neck-and-how-can-a-pillow-help-fix-it-in-singapore), and try our [posture habits and exercises](/blog/improve-good-posture-habits-with-exercises-and-home-remedies).
- **Your mattress.** A soft or sagging mattress changes how far your shoulder sinks. Bed firmness is one of the questions in Mpillow's configurator.
- **An injury.** Pain after a car accident or a fall needs a medical check, not a new pillow.

Your pillow is worth checking first if your neck is stiffest when you wake, if the pain started after a new pillow or mattress, or if it eases when you sleep somewhere else.

Loud snoring, gasping or choking at night, and feeling exhausted during the day can be signs of sleep apnoea, which needs a doctor rather than a new pillow. [[cite: NHS sleep apnoea: main symptoms; see a GP | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/]] If you already use CPAP, our page on [pillows for CPAP users](/blog/how-a-customised-pillow-can-help-with-sleep-apnea-for-cpap-users) explains what a pillow can and cannot do.

## When should you see a doctor about neck or shoulder pain?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if neck pain is not easing after a few weeks, keeps coming back, or stops you sleeping or working. HealthHub Singapore says most neck pain improves within about six weeks, and to see a doctor if it gets worse. [[cite: HealthHub Singapore neck pain: most improves within 6 weeks; see a doctor if it worsens | https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain]]

See a GP or physiotherapist soon if you have:

- neck pain that is getting worse, or has not improved after a few weeks of self-care
- pain that spreads down your arm
- numbness, tingling or pins and needles in your arms or hands
- weakness in an arm or hand [[cite: NHS neck pain: when to see a GP (pain not improving, pins and needles, numbness, weakness) | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neck-pain/]]

Get urgent help, at A&E or by calling 995, if:

- the pain started after a serious accident, fall or blow to the head
- neck pain comes with a high temperature and you feel very unwell
- you have new weakness in your arms or legs, or trouble walking [[cite: NHS neck pain urgent and emergency signs; Mayo Clinic: emergency care after traumatic injury | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neck-pain/]] [[cite: SCDF: call 995 for life-threatening emergencies such as major trauma | https://www.scdf.gov.sg/home/about-us/information-on-ems]]

A pillow is for comfort and support. It does not replace a diagnosis.

## Frequently asked questions

### What pillow height is best for neck pain?

There is no single right height. Side sleepers need enough height to keep the head level above the shoulder, so broader shoulders need a higher pillow. Back sleepers need a lower pillow that supports the curve of the neck.

### Can a pillow cause shoulder pain?

A pillow that is too low for side sleeping lets your head drop towards the mattress, and many people with one sore shoulder sleep on that side. Try sleeping on the other side or your back, check your pillow height, and see a GP if the pain lasts more than two weeks.

### Is memory foam good for neck pain?

Memory foam can work if the height suits your build and sleep position. Research suggests a pillow's shape and height affect neck alignment more than its material.

### Do I need a cervical pillow for neck pain?

Not necessarily. A contour shape on its own has not been shown to beat a well-fitted regular pillow, so getting the height right for your shoulders and sleep position matters more.

### Is it better to sleep on your back or side with neck pain?

Both can work with the right pillow height, and they are the two positions Mpillow recommends. Sleeping on your stomach keeps your neck turned for hours, so it is the position to move away from.

### How do I know if my pillow is too high?

On your side, your head tilts up towards the ceiling. On your back, your chin is pushed towards your chest.
