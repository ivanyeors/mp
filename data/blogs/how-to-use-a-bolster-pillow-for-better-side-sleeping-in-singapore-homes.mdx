You start the night on your side and wake up twisted, with your top knee on the mattress and the bolster on the floor. Or you sleep on your stomach, wake with a stiff neck, and cannot seem to stop. This page shows where to put a bolster in each sleep position and the mistakes that undo it. It also covers when pain needs more than a better set-up. For choosing a bolster's size, firmness and filling, see our [complete bolster guide](/blog/the-complete-guide-to-choosing-the-right-bolster-in-singapore).

## How do you position a bolster for side sleeping?

Hug the bolster in front of you at chest height, rest your top arm on it, and bring its lower end between your knees. Keep your head on a pillow high enough to hold your neck level. The aim is a straight line from your ear to your hip, with no twist at the waist.

Step by step:

1. **Lie on your side and bend your knees slightly.** Mayo Clinic recommends this position, with a pillow between the knees to keep the spine, pelvis and hips aligned. [[cite: Side sleepers: draw knees up slightly and place a pillow between them to align spine, pelvis and hips | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]]
2. **Place the top of the bolster under your top arm,** level with your chest. Your top shoulder should rest on it, not hunch up.
3. **Run the lower end between your knees.** If the bolster is long enough, let it continue down between your ankles too.
4. **Settle your head on its own pillow.** The pillow fills the gap your shoulder creates; the bolster cannot do that.
5. **Check your top knee.** It should sit roughly level with your hip, neither dropped onto the mattress nor lifted above it.

A pillow between the knees also stops your upper leg pulling forward and twisting your torso. [[cite: A pillow between the knees helps prevent the upper leg from pulling forward and twisting the torso, keeping hips and spine aligned | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/best-sleeping-positions-for-pain]]

Your head pillow matters as much as the bolster. Our [side sleeper pillow guide](/blog/side-sleeper-pillow-your-ultimate-guide-for-alignment-pain-relief) explains how to get its height right for your shoulder width.

## How do you use a bolster if you sleep on your back?

On your back, place the bolster under your knees. A pillow under the knees may help your back muscles relax and keep the natural curve of your lower back. [[cite: Back sleepers: a pillow under the knees may relax back muscles and maintain the lower back's curve | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]] Mpillow's own advice is similar: back sleepers may need support for the lower back.

Two other uses suit back sleepers:

- **Along your side,** as a rest for your arm, or as a gentle barrier if you tend to roll over.
- **As a habit bridge,** if you are moving from side to back sleeping and miss having something to hold.

Do not use a bolster in place of your head pillow. A narrow, round cushion rolls as you move, and it cannot hold your head at a steady height.

## Can stomach sleepers use a bolster?

Yes, but the most useful thing a bolster can do for a stomach sleeper is help them move onto their side. Sleeping face down keeps your head turned to one side for hours. Mayo Clinic describes stomach sleeping as hard on the back, and Cleveland Clinic says it can put extra stress on the back, neck and shoulders. [[cite: Stomach sleeping can be hard on the back; try other positions if possible | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]] [[cite: Sleeping face down can put extra stress on the back, neck and shoulders | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sleeping-on-stomach]]

Mpillow's own guidance points the same way. We recommend side and back sleeping, and we suggest stomach sleepers try moving to their side.

### First, try a part-turned position

Lie partly on your side, close to face down, with a bolster hugged along your front. The bolster gives the pressure against your chest and stomach that stomach sleepers are used to, while your head and hips stay closer to side-on. [[cite: Body pillows can help habitual stomach sleepers by giving pressure against the stomach while keeping them side-lying | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/is-your-pillow-hurting-your-neck-7-tips-for-better-sleep]] A pillow behind your back can act as a bumper, so you do not roll back onto your front. [[cite: Well-placed pillows act as bumpers to stop rolling onto the stomach; over time the body can be trained to stay on the side or back | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sleeping-on-stomach]]

### If you still sleep on your stomach

If you cannot sleep any other way, a pillow under your hips and lower stomach can reduce strain on your back. [[cite: Stomach sleepers: a pillow under the hips and lower stomach reduces back strain | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852]] A thin head pillow, or none at all, stops your neck bending backwards as well as turning. [[cite: Stomach sleepers should use a thin pillow or none to limit neck extension | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sleeping-on-stomach]]

### The two-Mbolster set-up

Mpillow has suggested one more option for committed stomach sleepers. Place one Mbolster on your left and one on your right, hug both, and keep your head on its own pillow. The idea is to keep your body balanced, so it does not twist towards one side.

Be clear about what this is. It is a comfort option, and no research has tested it. It will not make stomach sleeping as easy on your neck as side sleeping. Use it while you work towards sleeping on your side.

## What are the most common bolster mistakes?

Most bolster mistakes come from the wrong size, the wrong firmness or the wrong place. Each one is easy to correct once you know what to look for.

| Mistake | What happens | What to do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bolster too short | It supports your arm or your knees, not both | Choose a length that suits your height |
| Too thick between the knees | It lifts your top leg and tilts your pelvis | Use a slimmer bolster, or adjust a modular one |
| Too soft | It flattens overnight and your knees end up touching | Choose a firmer fill, or adjust a modular one |
| Hugged too high | It pushes your top shoulder towards your ear | Keep it at chest height |
| Used as a head pillow | Your neck rests on a narrow, rolling surface | Keep a proper pillow for your head |
| Pulled under your body | It twists your torso towards the mattress | Keep it in front of you |
| Rarely washed | It holds months of sweat and dust | Wash the case often and the inner regularly |

## What size and firmness work best for side sleeping?

For side sleeping, choose a bolster that reaches from your chest to your knees. It should be firm enough to hold your top knee level with your hip. A bolster that is too firm or too thick props your arm and knee up too high; one that is too soft collapses.

## When should you see a doctor or physiotherapist?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if neck, shoulder, back or hip pain carries on despite changes to how you sleep. Get checked promptly if pain comes with numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg, or if it started after a fall or injury.

A bolster can make a sleeping position more comfortable. It cannot find or resolve the cause of pain, and changing your set-up should not delay getting checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I hug my bolster or put it between my knees?

Do both if the bolster is long enough. Hug the top end at chest height to rest your arm, and run the lower end between your knees to keep your hips level.

### Can I use two bolsters at once?

Yes. Some side sleepers hug one and place a second behind their back to stop rolling over. Some stomach sleepers use one on each side to keep their body from twisting.

### Is it bad to sleep on your stomach?

Stomach sleeping keeps your head turned to one side for hours, which can strain your neck and lower back. Mpillow recommends trying side or back sleeping instead.

### How do I stop rolling onto my stomach at night?

Hug a bolster along your front and place a pillow behind your back, so you have something to lean into on both sides. Changing a long-held sleep habit takes time, so expect some nights back on your front at first.

### Can a bolster replace my head pillow?

No. A bolster supports your body, not your head. Your neck needs a proper pillow at a height that suits how you sleep.
