You wake with a stiff neck, or turn your pillow over at 3am looking for the cool side. The pillow may simply be wrong for you, rather than bad in general. Fit is personal: the same pillow can suit one person and leave their partner sore.

This guide takes the decision in the order that matters. First how you sleep, then height and firmness, then filling, then keeping cool and clean in Singapore's climate. It ends with when a custom pillow is worth paying for, and when it is not.

## What decides whether a pillow suits you?

Three things decide it: the height your head rests at, how well the pillow keeps that height overnight, and what it is filled with. Brand, price and the word "ergonomic" on the label matter far less.

The goal is the same for every sleeper. Your head should stay in line with your spine, neither tipped down towards the mattress nor pushed up. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/in-depth/sleeping-positions/art-20546852) puts it simply: your pillow should keep your neck in line with your chest and back. What changes from person to person is how much pillow it takes to get there.

Research has not settled on one ideal pillow height. A [2021 review of pillow-height studies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8544534/) found that body size, neck alignment, contact pressure and muscle activity all play a part. The evidence could not yet identify an optimal range. So a pillow that suits a friend tells you little about whether it will suit you.

## How does your sleep position change the pillow you need?

Your sleep position sets the height you need more than anything else. Side sleeping needs the most height, back sleeping a moderate amount, and stomach sleeping the least.

| Sleep position | What the pillow has to do | Height | Firmness | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Side | Fill the gap between your ear and the outside of your shoulder | Highest | Medium-firm to firm | A pillow that sinks, so the head drops towards the mattress |
| Back | Support the curve of the neck without pushing the chin forward | Medium | Medium | Stacking two pillows, which bends the neck forward |
| Stomach | Keep the head close to flat while it is turned to one side | Lowest, or none | Soft | Any pillow thick enough to tilt the head back |
| Side and back | Rise at the edges for side sleeping and dip in the centre for back sleeping | Two heights in one pillow | Medium | A flat, even pillow that is too low on your side or too high on your back |

### Side sleepers

When you lie on your side, your shoulder holds your upper body off the mattress. The pillow has to make up that distance, so broad shoulders need more height than narrow ones. Our guide to the [best pillow for side sleepers](/blog/side-sleeper-pillow-your-ultimate-guide-for-alignment-pain-relief) covers this in detail, including what to do with your knees.

### Back sleepers

On your back, your head needs less lift. The hollow behind your neck still needs filling, so look for a pillow a little fuller under the neck than under the head. [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/say-good-night-to-neck-pain) suggests a pillow with built-in neck support and a hollow for the head, or a small neck roll inside a flatter pillow. If your chin drifts towards your chest, the pillow is too high.

### Stomach sleepers

Face down, your head has to turn fully to one side so you can breathe. Holding that twist for hours pulls your body out of its natural alignment ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sleeping-on-stomach)). A very thin pillow, or none, at least stops the head tilting back as well. Mpillow's own advice to stomach sleepers is to try moving to side sleeping.

### Combination sleepers

If you start the night on your back and end it on your side, a pillow of one even height cannot suit both. Look for a pillow that is higher at the edges than in the centre, or one you can adjust section by section.

## How high should your pillow be?

Your pillow is the right height when your head stays level with your spine in your usual position. Shoulder width, mattress softness and head shape all change how much pillow that takes.

A simple check works better than any chart. Lie in your normal position for a few minutes, then ask someone to photograph you from the foot of the bed.

- **On your side:** the line from your nose to your breastbone should run level with the mattress. If your head tips down, the pillow is too low. If it tips up, it is too high.
- **On your back:** your face should point at the ceiling, neither tucked towards your chest nor tilted back.
- **Either way:** sliding a hand under the pillow, or folding it in half at night, usually means it lacks height.

Your mattress changes the answer. A soft mattress lets your shoulder sink, which narrows the gap, so you need a little less pillow. A firm mattress keeps your shoulder up, so you need a little more. The Premium Mpillow configurator asks how firm your bed is, alongside your measurements.

## How firm should a pillow be?

A pillow should be firm enough to keep its height under the weight of your head all night. How soft the surface feels is a separate question of comfort.

A pillow that starts at the right height but slowly sinks will be too low by morning. That is how a soft pillow lets a side sleeper down. Firmness and height also trade off: a tall, soft pillow and a lower, firmer one can hold your head at the same height.

Test a pillow for five minutes in your usual position, not five seconds. If your head keeps sinking, or you feel the urge to bunch the pillow up, it is too soft for you.

## What are the main types of pillow filling?

The main pillow fillings are memory foam, latex, polyester or microfibre, down and feather, buckwheat, and modular pouches. Each trades support, airflow and ease of washing differently.

| Filling | Feel | Holds its height | Airflow | Washing | Main drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Memory foam block | Slow, moulding, dense | Well | Low; can feel warm | Cover only; the foam core does not go in the machine | One fixed height you cannot change |
| Shredded memory foam | Mouldable, softer than a block | Fairly well | Better than a block | Check the label | Pieces shift and clump |
| Latex | Springy, pushes back | Well | Good, especially with pin-hole cores | Cover only; spot-clean the core | Heavy; unsuitable for latex allergy |
| Polyester or microfibre | Soft, light | Poorly over time | Moderate | Often machine washable | Flattens and lumps sooner |
| Down and feather | Very soft, squashable | Poorly; needs plumping | Moderate | Some are washable but slow to dry | Little structure for side sleepers |
| Buckwheat hulls | Firm, mouldable, rustles | Well | Good | Case only; hulls are aired or topped up | Heavy and noisy |
| Modular pouches | Depends on the mix | Well, and adjustable | Depends on the fibres | Components wash separately | Needs fitting to get right |

### What does research say about filling type?

Only a few trials have compared fillings, and they are small. In [one Australian trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3004642/), side sleepers tried five pillow types for a week each. A latex pillow did best for waking symptoms, and a feather pillow did worst.

With so little evidence on fillings, choose one for comfort and practicality, but get the height right first.

### What should you know about each filling?

- **Memory foam** moulds to your head and holds a steady height. Dense foam holds warmth, though, and a block's height cannot be changed if it is wrong for you.
- **Latex** is springy and keeps its shape. It is heavy, and [Allergy UK](https://www.allergyuk.org/resources/rubber-latex-allergy/) lists latex pillows among everyday latex products. If you have a latex allergy, ask your doctor before choosing one.
- **Polyester and microfibre** are light and easy to wash, but loose fibre shifts and flattens. Fibre packed into separate compartments or pouches holds its shape better.
- **Down and feather** suit people who like to scrunch a soft pillow, but give little structure for side sleepers and need plumping.
- **Buckwheat** gives firm, mouldable support with air between the hulls, but is heavy and noisy. [Anaphylaxis UK](https://anaphylaxis.org.uk/factsheets/buckwheat-allergy/) describes cases where sleeping on a buckwheat pillow was linked to buckwheat allergy.
- **Modular pouches** let height and feel change piece by piece. The Mpillow mixes softer hollow-fibre pouches and firmer fibre-ball pouches across three compartments. Our team confirms the mix for each pillow; you choose the cover and the case.

## How do you sleep cooler in Singapore's humidity?

Pick a pillow that lets air move, use a case that copes with sweat, and keep both clean. In Singapore the problem is humidity as much as heat, because sweat evaporates slowly in humid air.

A [review of sleep studies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3427038/) found that heat increases time awake and reduces deep and REM sleep. Humid heat adds further to the body's heat load in sleep. A pillow will not cool a room, but it can avoid adding to the problem.

- **Choose airflow over density.** A solid memory foam block holds warmth against your head. Latex with pin holes, buckwheat and fibre pouches leave more room for air.
- **Use a breathable case.** Cotton and linen breathe and wash easily. The Premium Mpillow comes with a linen case or a FibreCool case, which we describe as helpful if you run warm.
- **Wash the case often.** A spare case makes weekly washing easy.
- **Dry the filling right through.** Filling that stays damp after washing will start to smell, so wash on a dry, sunny day.
- **Keep sweat out of the filling.** The Mpillow's inner protector is waterproof, with filter holes to let air through.

Dust mites also thrive in humid homes, according to [Allergy UK](https://www.allergyuk.org/resources/house-dust-mite-allergy-factsheet/). That is one more reason to choose a pillow whose parts you can wash. If you have a dust mite allergy, ask your doctor whether allergen-proof covers would help.

## How long should a pillow last?

A pillow is due for replacement when it no longer holds the height you need, whatever its age. Fillings wear at different rates, and with a modular pillow, worn parts can be replaced instead of the whole pillow.

## What pillow should a child use?

Babies under 12 months should not sleep with a pillow at all, as the [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/a-parents-guide-to-safe-sleep.aspx) advises. Older children have narrower shoulders than adults, so they need a lower pillow, and the right height changes as they grow. ## What if you already have neck pain?

The right pillow can reduce strain on your neck at night, but it does not remove the cause of neck pain. See a doctor or physiotherapist if pain lasts more than a few weeks or keeps coming back. Do the same after an injury, or if you have numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/neck-pain-and-stiff-neck/) advises seeing a GP for neck pain that lasts beyond a few weeks or comes with pins and needles. Its [whiplash advice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/whiplash/) asks for an urgent appointment if your arms feel weak or tingle after a neck injury.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my mattress change the pillow height I need?

Yes. A soft mattress lets your shoulder sink, so a side sleeper needs a little less pillow. A firm mattress keeps the shoulder up, so you need a little more.

### Is memory foam a good choice if I sleep hot?

A solid memory foam block is dense and lets little air through, so it tends to hold warmth. If you run warm, shredded foam, latex with pin holes, buckwheat or fibre pouches are usually cooler choices.

### Which pillow filling is easiest to keep clean?

Fibre fillings and modular pouches are usually easiest, because the filling itself can be washed. Memory foam and latex cores cannot go in the washing machine, so you rely on washing the cover.

### Which pillow filling holds its height best?

Latex, foam, buckwheat and fibre packed into separate pouches hold their height best. Loose polyester and feather compress the most, so they need plumping and lose height sooner.

### Should a stomach sleeper use a pillow?

A stomach sleeper needs a very thin, soft pillow or none, so the head is not tilted back as well as turned. Mpillow recommends trying to move to side sleeping.
