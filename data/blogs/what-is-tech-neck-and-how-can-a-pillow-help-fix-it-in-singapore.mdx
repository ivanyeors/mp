A day of laptop work, then a commute spent scrolling on the MRT, can leave your neck and shoulders tight by evening. "Tech neck" is the name many people now give that feeling.

This page explains what tech neck is and why looking down strains the neck. It covers the daytime habits that help most, and what your pillow can and cannot do. ## What is tech neck?

Tech neck, or text neck, describes neck and shoulder strain from holding your head tilted down at a screen for long periods. It is a descriptive term for an overuse problem, not a formal medical diagnosis. [[cite: SingHealth HealthXchange: text neck is an overuse strain from prolonged forward head tilt at devices | https://www.healthxchange.sg/head-neck/neck-thyroid/text-neck-syndrome-symptoms-prevention]]

Symptoms commonly linked to tech neck include:

- soreness or stiffness in the neck
- tight shoulders and upper back
- headaches
- discomfort that builds through the day [[cite: HealthXchange and Cleveland Clinic: tech neck symptoms - neck soreness, shoulder tightness, headaches | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/tech-neck]]

## Why does looking down strain your neck?

When your head tips forward, the muscles at the back of your neck and upper back work harder to hold it up. The further it tips, the greater the load. [[cite: SingHealth HealthXchange: leaning the head forward from neutral increases the load the neck must carry | https://www.healthxchange.sg/head-neck/neck-thyroid/text-neck-syndrome-symptoms-prevention]]

A widely quoted computer model estimated that the force on the neck rises steeply as the head bends forward. [[cite: Hansraj 2014, Surg Technol Int: modelled cervical spine load increases with forward head flexion angle | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25393825/]] It was a model rather than a measurement in people. Still, it shows why long spells with your head down get attention.

## Does phone use really cause neck pain?

The evidence is mixed. Some studies link heavy device use with neck pain, while others find no clear link with phone posture itself.

- **No link with texting posture:** in a study of 150 young adults in Brazil, texting posture was not associated with neck pain. [[cite: Damasceno et al. 2018, Eur Spine J: text neck posture not associated with neck pain in 150 young adults aged 18-21 | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-017-5444-5]]
- **Common in Singapore:** a SingHealth-reported survey of young adults in Singapore found six in 10 had neck or back pain after using their devices. [[cite: HealthXchange: Singapore survey, six in 10 young adults had neck and back pain after device use | https://www.healthxchange.sg/head-neck/neck-thyroid/text-neck-syndrome-symptoms-prevention]]

One reading of this: the angle of your phone may matter less than how long you stay still. Some physiotherapy researchers argue that no single posture is ideal, and that changing position often is what helps. [[cite: Slater et al. 2019 JOSPT "Sit up straight: time to re-evaluate" (NHS South Tees leaflet based on it): no single optimal posture; changing position helps | https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Posture-Reformed-A5-4pp-V2.pdf]]

## Which daytime habits help tech neck most?

Bring screens up towards your eyes, break up long sessions, and keep your neck and upper back moving. These habits act on the cause, while a pillow only affects the hours you sleep.

- **Lift your phone.** Hold it up closer to eye level instead of bending your neck to look down at it. [[cite: Mayo Clinic neck pain prevention: hold small screens up rather than bending the neck to look down | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581]]
- **Raise your monitor.** Set the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away. [[cite: Mayo Clinic office ergonomics: top of monitor at or slightly below eye level, about arm's length away (search found the Mayo Clinic Care Network copy; prefer the mayoclinic.org original) | https://mymlc.com/health-information/articles/o/office-ergonomics-your-how-to-guide]]
- **Give your laptop a stand.** A stand with a separate keyboard and mouse lets the screen sit higher. [[cite: Mayo Clinic office ergonomics: laptop with external keyboard, mouse and stand | https://mymlc.com/health-information/articles/o/office-ergonomics-your-how-to-guide]]
- **Take breaks.** Singapore General Hospital suggests a break from sitting about every 30 minutes. [[cite: SGH office ergonomics: break from sitting every 30 minutes | https://www.sgh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/office-ergonomics-occupational-overuse]]
- **Move your neck and shoulders.** Use those breaks for chin tucks, neck turns and shoulder rolls. Our [posture habits and exercises](/blog/improve-good-posture-habits-with-exercises-and-home-remedies) show how to do them.
- **Build strength over time.** For long-lasting neck pain, exercise that strengthens the neck, shoulders and upper back has the best research support. [[cite: Gross et al. 2015 Cochrane review: moderate-quality evidence for cervico-scapulothoracic strengthening in chronic neck pain | https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004250.pub5/information]]

| Situation | Habit to try |
| --- | --- |
| On the MRT or bus | Hold your phone higher, and look up between stops |
| Laptop on the sofa | Move to a table, or raise the laptop and use a separate keyboard |
| Office desk | Raise the monitor and stand up every half hour |
| Homework on a tablet | Use a stand so the screen sits higher |
| Scrolling in bed | Put the phone away, rather than propping your head on stacked pillows |

## What about children and teenagers?

Children pick up the same screen habits, and the same simple measures apply. A review of school-age children linked phone use and heavy backpacks with neck and upper-body symptoms, though the research is still limited. [[cite: 2023 review, school-aged children: neck and upper extremity symptoms with cell phone and backpack postures | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10048647/]]

Practical steps for families:

- prop tablets on a stand for homework and video calls
- break up long gaming or study sessions with time to move
- check that the desk and chair suit your child's size, not an adult's
- see a doctor if a child's neck or back pain lasts or keeps coming back

## Can a pillow help with tech neck?

A pillow cannot reverse tech neck, but the wrong one can add hours of strain on top of a long screen day. The aim at night is a neutral neck, with your head in line with your spine. [[cite: Mayo Clinic neck pain prevention: keep head and neck aligned with the body when sleeping | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581]]

- **Sleep on your side or back.** HealthHub Singapore advises people with neck pain to avoid sleeping on their stomach. [[cite: HealthHub Singapore neck pain: avoid sleeping on your stomach | https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain]]
- **Match the height to your position.** Side sleeping needs more height, set by your shoulder width. Back sleeping needs less, with support under the neck. Our [guide to cervical support pillows](/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-cervical-support-pillows-for-neck-pain-relief) explains how contour pillows fit in.
- **Stop scrolling once you lie down.** Propping your head on stacked pillows to watch a screen repeats the forward bend of the day.

## When should you see a doctor or physiotherapist?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if neck pain lasts more than a few weeks, keeps returning, or is getting worse despite changing your habits. Get help sooner for pain that spreads into your arm, or for numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands. New or severe headaches need checking too. [[cite: Mayo Clinic neck pain: when to see a doctor (persists after several weeks, radiates into arms, with numbness, tingling, weakness or headache) | https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581]]

## Frequently asked questions

### Is tech neck a real medical condition?

Tech neck is a popular term for neck and shoulder strain from screen posture, not a formal diagnosis. The symptoms are real, and daytime habits are the first thing to change.

### Can tech neck cause headaches?

Headaches are among the symptoms commonly linked to text neck. A headache that is new, severe or persistent should be checked by a doctor.

### What is the best sleeping position for tech neck?

Sleep on your side or back, with a pillow that keeps your head in line with your spine. Stomach sleeping keeps your neck turned for hours.

### Will a cervical pillow get rid of tech neck?

No pillow can undo strain from the day. A well-fitted cervical pillow supports your neck overnight, so sleep does not add to it.

### How often should I take a break from my screen?

Singapore General Hospital suggests a break from sitting about every 30 minutes. Use it to stand up, walk a few steps, and move your neck and shoulders.
