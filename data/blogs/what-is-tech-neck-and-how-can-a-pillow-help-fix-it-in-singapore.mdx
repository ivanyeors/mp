A day of laptop work, then a commute spent scrolling on the MRT, can leave your neck and shoulders tight by evening. "Tech neck" is the name many people now give that feeling.

This page explains what tech neck is and why looking down strains the neck. It covers the daytime habits that help most, and what your pillow can and cannot do. ## What is tech neck?

Tech neck, or text neck, describes neck and shoulder strain from holding your head tilted down at a screen for long periods. SingHealth's [HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/how-to-prevent/neck-pain/text-neck-syndrome-symptoms-prevention) calls it a repetitive stress injury from prolonged forward head posture while using mobile devices. It is a descriptive term for an overuse problem, not a formal medical diagnosis.

Symptoms that [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/tech-neck) and HealthXchange link to tech neck include:

- soreness, stiffness or pain in the neck and shoulders
- tightness across the shoulders
- headaches

## Why does looking down strain your neck?

As your head tips forward, the load on your neck increases and your neck muscles have to work harder to hold it up. A Singapore General Hospital physiotherapist explains this on [HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/how-to-manage/neck-pain/tech-neck-text-neck-when-to-see-doctor-for-neck-pain).

A widely quoted [2014 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25393825/) estimated that the load on the neck rises as the head bends further forward. It was a computer model of load on the spine, not a measurement of pain or harm in people ([a later study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4777436/)). Still, it shows why long spells with your head down get attention.

## Does phone use really cause neck pain?

The evidence is mixed. Some studies link heavy device use with neck pain, while others find no clear link with phone posture itself.

- **No link with texting posture:** in a [2018 study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-017-5444-5) of 150 young adults in Brazil, texting posture was not associated with neck pain.
- **Common in Singapore:** in a local survey of over 500 young adults, six in 10 had neck and back pain after device use ([HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/news/few-know-about-text-neck)).

One reading of this: the angle of your phone may matter less than how long you stay still. In a [2019 journal viewpoint](https://www.jospt.org/doi/abs/10.2519/jospt.2019.0610), physiotherapy researchers argued that no one posture has been shown to prevent pain, and that changing position can help. An [NHS trust's posture leaflet](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Posture-Reformed-A5-4pp-V2.pdf) says much the same.

## Which daytime habits help tech neck most?

Bring screens up towards your eyes, break up long sessions, and keep your neck and upper back moving. These habits act on the cause, while a pillow only affects the hours you sleep.

- **Lift your phone.** Keep your head up and hold the phone out in front of you, rather than bending your neck to look down ([Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/neck-pain/symptoms-causes/syc-20375581)).
- **Raise your screens.** Set the top of your monitor at or slightly below eye level, as [Mayo Clinic's office ergonomics guide](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/office-ergonomics/art-20046169) advises. Give a laptop a stand, with a separate keyboard and mouse.
- **Take breaks.** The Singapore General Hospital physiotherapist on HealthXchange suggests regular movement breaks every 30 minutes or so.
- **Move your neck and shoulders.** Use those breaks for chin tucks, neck turns and shoulder rolls. Our [posture habits and exercises](/blog/improve-good-posture-habits-with-exercises-and-home-remedies) show how to do them.
- **Build strength over time.** For long-lasting neck pain, a [Cochrane review](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004250.pub5/abstract) found that strengthening the neck, shoulders and upper back eased pain in the short term.

| Situation | Habit to try |
| --- | --- |
| On the MRT or bus | Hold your phone higher, and look up between stops |
| Laptop on the sofa | Move to a table, or raise the laptop and use a separate keyboard |
| Office desk | Raise the monitor and stand up every half hour |
| Back-to-back video calls | Stand up or stretch between calls |

## What about children and teenagers?

Children pick up the same screen habits, and the same simple measures apply. A [2023 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10048647/) linked phone use in children and teenagers with neck and shoulder discomfort.

Practical steps for families:

- prop tablets on a stand for homework and video calls
- break up long gaming or study sessions with time to move
- check that the desk and chair suit your child's size, not an adult's
- see a doctor if a child's neck or back pain lasts or keeps coming back

## Can a pillow help with tech neck?

A pillow cannot reverse tech neck, but the wrong one can add hours of strain on top of a long screen day. The aim at night is to keep your head and neck in line with your body, as Mayo Clinic advises.

- **Sleep on your side or back.** [HealthHub Singapore](https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/neck-pain) advises people with neck pain to try not to sleep on their stomach.
- **Match the height to your position.** Side sleeping needs more height, set by your shoulder width. Back sleeping needs less, with support under the neck. Our [guide to cervical support pillows](/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-cervical-support-pillows-for-neck-pain-relief) explains how contour pillows fit in.
- **Stop scrolling once you lie down.** Propping your head on stacked pillows to watch a screen repeats the forward bend of the day.

## When should you see a doctor or physiotherapist?

See a doctor or physiotherapist if neck pain lasts more than a few weeks, keeps returning, or is getting worse despite changing your habits. Get help sooner for pain that spreads into your arm, or for numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands. New, severe or worsening headaches need checking too. HealthXchange gives similar warning signs for tech neck.

Get urgent help if the pain follows an accident or fall. Our neck pain guide lists [the warning signs in full](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore#when-should-you-see-a-doctor-about-neck-or-shoulder-pain).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is tech neck a real medical condition?

Tech neck is a popular term for neck and shoulder strain from screen posture, not a formal diagnosis. The symptoms are real, and daytime habits are the first thing to change.

### Can tech neck cause headaches?

Headaches are among the symptoms commonly linked to text neck. A headache that is new, severe or persistent should be checked by a doctor.

### Can children get tech neck?

Children use the same screens, and a 2023 review linked phone use in children and teenagers with neck and shoulder discomfort. Prop tablets on a stand, and see a doctor if a child's neck pain lasts.

### Will a cervical pillow get rid of tech neck?

No pillow can undo strain from the day. A well-fitted cervical pillow supports your neck overnight, so sleep does not add to it.

### How often should I take a break from my screen?

A Singapore General Hospital physiotherapist suggests a movement break every 30 minutes or so. Use it to stand up, walk a few steps, and move your neck and shoulders.
