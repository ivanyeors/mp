If you use CPAP, you may know the hiss of a leak as you roll onto your side. Or you wake with the mask pushed across your cheek. It is tempting to blame the pillow, and sometimes it does play a part.

This page explains what a pillow can and cannot do for CPAP users and what the evidence says about CPAP pillows. It also covers when to talk to your doctor. It is part of our guide to [the best pillow for neck pain](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore).

This is general information, not medical advice. Keep to the treatment your doctor or sleep clinic has set, and do not change it because of anything you read here.

## Can a pillow help with sleep apnoea?

Not as a treatment. A pillow does not treat sleep apnoea, and it is not a substitute for CPAP or any other treatment your doctor recommends.

The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/) describes sleep apnoea as breathing that stops and starts while you sleep. The most common type is obstructive sleep apnoea.

Many people with sleep apnoea need a CPAP machine. It gently pumps air into a mask worn over the nose, or the nose and mouth, during sleep. The NHS says it works best if you use it every night. If you find it uncomfortable or hard to use, the NHS advice is to tell your doctor.

The US [National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea/causes) says sleep apnoea may raise the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke. That is why treatment matters far more than any pillow. Be wary of any pillow sold as a sleep apnoea treatment.

## Why does a CPAP mask leak when you sleep on your side?

Sleeping off your back, which includes lying on your side, is one of several things linked to unintended mask leaks. In [a study of CPAP users](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28847549/), leaks were more likely in positions other than on the back. They were also more likely with the mouth open, at higher pressures and during REM sleep.

On your side, the mask sits close to the pillow. A pillow that is too high, too soft or bulky at the edge can press on the mask or tug the hose. Either may lift the seal.

A leak is a reason to call your sleep clinic or equipment provider, not to stop CPAP. They can check the fit, the cushion and the mask style.

## Do CPAP pillows with cut-outs work?

The evidence is thin. A [small 2016 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27812636) followed 22 adults starting CPAP. Compared with their own pillows, pillows with side cut-outs did not change air leaks, pressures, remaining breathing events or hours of use.

Participants did prefer a gel pillow with cut-outs. So comfort may differ even when the CPAP readings do not.

That makes a cut-out pillow a comfort choice, not a therapy upgrade. The pillow that suits you is one that keeps your head level and leaves the mask alone.

## What should a CPAP user look for in a pillow?

The same height and support anyone needs, plus room for the mask. A [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/is-your-pillow-hurting-your-neck-7-tips-for-better-sleep) chiropractor names a pillow that bends your neck forward or to one side as the most common pillow mistake.

| What to check | Why it matters with a mask | Who can help |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height on your side | Too high can push the mask into the pillow; too low lets the face drop onto it | A pillow fitter |
| Height on your back | Too high tucks the chin towards the chest | A pillow fitter |
| Firmness | A very soft pillow lets the face sink in around the mask | A pillow fitter |
| Mask style and fit | Different mask styles sit differently against a pillow | Your sleep clinic or equipment provider |
| Hose position | A hose dragged across the pillow can pull on the mask | Your sleep clinic or equipment provider |

Side sleepers need more height than back sleepers, because the pillow fills the gap between shoulder and head. For a sore neck, the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cervical-spondylosis/) advises seeing a GP if pain or stiffness does not go away after a few weeks. Ask for an urgent appointment if you also have weakness in your arms or pins and needles in an arm.

## Does sleeping on your side help sleep apnoea?

Only your doctor or sleep specialist can tell you whether it does in your case. Sleep position is not a substitute for CPAP or any other treatment your doctor recommends.

If you want to know whether position plays a part in your sleep apnoea, ask your sleep specialist. Ask them before using any pillow or device to change how you sleep.

A bolster can make side lying more comfortable, but it is not a positional therapy device. Our guide to [using a bolster for side sleeping](/blog/how-to-use-a-bolster-pillow-for-better-side-sleeping-in-singapore-homes) covers how to position one.

## When should you see a doctor about sleep apnoea?

The NHS lists the main symptoms of sleep apnoea. They include breathing that stops and starts, gasping, snorting or choking noises, waking up a lot, and loud snoring. See a GP if you or a partner notice any of these.

In Singapore, start with your GP, who can advise you on the next steps.

If you already use CPAP:

- Do not stop or change your settings because of discomfort. Tell your sleep clinic or doctor instead.
- Report leaks, a dry mouth, or a mask that moves during the night.
- Bring up neck or shoulder pain too, as the pillow and mask may need adjusting together.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Mpillow work with a CPAP mask?

Mpillow has no clinical data on the Mpillow with CPAP masks. Its side height is set from your shoulder length, and at an in-store fitting you can try it with your own mask.

### What is the best sleeping position with CPAP?

The position your sleep clinic is happy with and that you can sleep in. In one study, lying off your back was linked with more mask leaks. If you sleep on your side, ask your clinic whether your mask suits it.

### Do I need a special CPAP pillow?

Not necessarily. A small trial found pillows with cut-outs did not change leaks or CPAP use, though people liked a gel one. A pillow at the right height for your position matters more.

### Should I stop using CPAP if my mask keeps leaking?

No. Tell your sleep clinic or equipment provider, who can check the mask fit, cushion and style.

### Can a bolster keep me off my back?

A bolster can make side sleeping more comfortable, but it is not designed to stop you rolling onto your back. If your doctor suggests positional therapy, ask which device suits you.
