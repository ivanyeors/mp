If you use CPAP, you may know the hiss of a leak as you roll onto your side. Or you wake with the mask pushed across your cheek. It is tempting to blame the pillow, and sometimes the pillow is part of the problem.

This page explains what a pillow can and cannot do for CPAP users and what the evidence says about CPAP pillows. It also covers when to talk to your doctor. It is part of our guide to [the best pillow for neck pain](/blog/what-is-the-best-pillow-for-neck-pain-in-singapore).

This is general information, not medical advice. Keep to the treatment your doctor or sleep clinic has set, and do not change it because of anything you read here.

## Can a pillow help with sleep apnoea?

Not as a treatment. A pillow does not treat sleep apnoea, and it is not a substitute for CPAP or any other treatment your doctor recommends.

Obstructive sleep apnoea happens when the walls of the throat relax and narrow during sleep, so breathing repeatedly stops and starts. [[cite: obstructive sleep apnoea: throat walls relax and narrow during sleep, interrupting breathing; symptoms include breathing stopping and starting, gasping or choking noises, loud snoring, waking often, and feeling very sleepy during the day | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/]]

CPAP keeps the airway open by pumping air through a mask, and it works best when used every night. [[cite: CPAP gently pumps air into a mask worn over the nose, or nose and mouth; very effective if used every night; if it is uncomfortable, tell your doctor rather than stopping, as sleep services can adjust the mask and pressure | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/treatment/]]

Untreated sleep apnoea is linked to high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke. [[cite: sleep apnoea may raise the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke and diabetes | https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea]] That is why treatment matters far more than any pillow. Be wary of any pillow sold as a sleep apnoea treatment.

## Why does a CPAP mask leak when you sleep on your side?

Lying on your side is one of several things linked to unintended mask leaks. A study of CPAP users found leaks were more likely off the back, with the mouth open, at higher pressures and during REM sleep. [[cite: Lebret et al., Chest 2018: mouth opening, high CPAP pressure, body position other than supine and REM sleep increased the risk of unintentional leaks; oronasal masks reduced leaks with mouth opening and in REM | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28847549/]]

On your side, the mask sits close to the pillow. A pillow that is too high, too soft or bulky at the edge can press on the mask or tug the hose, which may lift the seal.

A leak is a reason to call your sleep clinic or equipment provider, not to stop CPAP. They can check the fit, the cushion and the mask style.

## Do CPAP pillows with cut-outs work?

The evidence is thin. A small Brazilian trial followed 22 adults starting CPAP. Compared with their own pillows, pillows with side cut-outs did not change air leaks, pressures, remaining breathing events or hours of use. [[cite: Salvaggio et al., J Bras Pneumol 2016: 22 completers; own pillow, then foam and gel pillows with side cut-outs, 5 nights each on auto-CPAP; no difference in pressures, residual AHI, air leaks or mean CPAP use; patients preferred the gel pillow | https://www.scielo.br/j/jbpneu/a/WD6Rh7dNMkhWMbLfKSNBSwC/?lang=en]]

Participants did prefer a gel pillow with cut-outs. So comfort may differ even when the CPAP readings do not.

That makes a cut-out pillow a comfort choice, not a therapy upgrade. The pillow that suits you is one that keeps your head level and leaves the mask alone.

## What should a CPAP user look for in a pillow?

The same height and support anyone needs, plus room for the mask. A Cleveland Clinic chiropractor names a pillow that bends your neck forward or to one side as the most common pillow mistake. [[cite: most common mistake is a pillow that bends the neck forward or to one side; too low points the jaw to the ceiling, too high points it to the chest | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/is-your-pillow-hurting-your-neck-7-tips-for-better-sleep]]

| What to check | Why it matters with a mask | Who can help |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height on your side | Too high can push the mask into the pillow; too low lets the face drop onto it | A pillow fitter |
| Height on your back | Too high tucks the chin towards the chest | A pillow fitter |
| Firmness | A very soft pillow lets the face sink in around the mask | A pillow fitter |
| Mask style and fit | Different mask styles sit differently against a pillow | Your sleep clinic or equipment provider |
| Hose position | A hose dragged across the pillow can pull on the mask | Your sleep clinic or equipment provider |

Side sleepers need more height than back sleepers, because the pillow fills the gap between shoulder and head. If your neck is sore as well, see a doctor or physiotherapist when pain lasts more than a few weeks. Go sooner if it comes with numbness, tingling or weakness. [[cite: see a GP if neck pain does not improve after a few weeks; get urgent advice for numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms, or pain after an injury | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neck-pain/]]

## Does sleeping on your side help sleep apnoea?

For some people it can, but only as part of a plan agreed with your doctor. In some people, apnoea is worse on their back. The NHS lists side-sleeping aids such as a tennis ball sewn into sleepwear or a special pillow. [[cite: NHS: try to sleep on your side, for example by taping a tennis ball to the back of your sleepwear, or with a special pillow or bed wedge | https://111.wales.nhs.uk/sleepapnoea/]]

A Cochrane review found CPAP reduced breathing pauses more than positional therapy did. People used positional devices for longer each night, and the trials were short. [[cite: Cochrane review 2019 (Srijithesh et al.): 8 trials lasting one night to two months; CPAP reduced AHI more than positional therapy; positional therapy was used about 2.5 hours a night longer; no clear difference in quality of life or sleep quality | https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010990.pub2/full]]

Whether your apnoea depends on position shows up on a sleep study. [[cite: AASM guideline recommends a sleep study to confirm position-dependent OSA before positional therapy | https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0101/p16.html]] Ask your sleep specialist before relying on position alone.

A bolster can make side lying more comfortable, but it is not a positional therapy device. Our guide to [using a bolster for side sleeping](/blog/how-to-use-a-bolster-pillow-for-better-side-sleeping-in-singapore-homes) covers how to position one.

## When should you see a doctor about sleep apnoea?

See a GP if a partner notices loud snoring, gasping or pauses in your breathing. The same applies if you wake unrefreshed and feel sleepy in the day. The NHS lists these among the main symptoms of sleep apnoea. [[cite: NHS sleep apnoea: main symptoms include breathing stopping and starting, gasping or choking, loud snoring, waking often and feeling very sleepy in the day | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/]]

In Singapore, a GP can refer you to a specialist, who may arrange a sleep study. [[cite: Singapore: a GP may refer the patient to a specialist, and a sleep study may be done to determine whether the patient has OSA | https://www.healthxchange.sg/news/all-about-osa]]

If you already use CPAP:

- Do not stop or change your settings because of discomfort. Tell your sleep clinic, which can adjust the mask or pressure.
- Report leaks, a dry mouth, or a mask that moves during the night.
- Bring up neck or shoulder pain too, as the pillow and mask may need adjusting together.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Mpillow work with a CPAP mask?

Mpillow has no clinical data on the Mpillow with CPAP masks. Its side height is set from your shoulder length, and at an in-store fitting you can ask to try it with your mask.

### What is the best sleeping position with CPAP?

The position your sleep clinic is happy with and that you can sleep in. Lying off your back is linked with more mask leaks, so if you sleep on your side, ask your clinic whether your mask suits it.

### Do I need a special CPAP pillow?

Not necessarily. A small trial found pillows with cut-outs did not change leaks or CPAP use, though people liked a gel one. A pillow at the right height for your position matters more.

### Should I stop using CPAP if my mask keeps leaking?

No. Tell your sleep clinic or equipment provider, who can check the mask fit, cushion and style.

### Can a bolster keep me off my back?

A bolster can make side sleeping more comfortable, but it is not designed to stop you rolling onto your back. If your doctor suggests positional therapy, ask which device suits you.
