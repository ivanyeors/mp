Your child ends up sideways in bed, sleeps on top of the pillow, or pushes it onto the floor. It is natural to wonder whether a better pillow would help, and when a small child should have one at all.

This page sets out when a pillow is safe, how high and firm a child's pillow should be, and what actually helps children sleep. It is part of our guide on [how to choose a pillow in Singapore](/blog/how-to-choose-the-best-pillow-in-singapore).

## When can a baby have a pillow?

Not before 12 months. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises that babies sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface. The sleep area should have no pillows, pillow-like toys, blankets or other soft objects. [[cite: AAP 2022 safe sleep recommendations: back to sleep on a firm, flat, non-inclined surface; keep pillows, pillow-like toys, quilts, comforters and loose bedding out of the infant's sleep area for the first year, to reduce SIDS and suffocation risk | https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/safe-sleep-back-is-best-avoid-soft-bedding-inclined-surfaces-and-bedsharing.aspx]]

Singapore's HealthHub gives the same advice for cots: no loose bedding, bumpers or soft toys, and a firm mattress that fits snugly. [[cite: HealthHub cot safety checklist: no loose objects in the cot (bedding, blankets, quilts, cot bumpers, soft toys); use a firm, snugly fitting mattress | https://ch-api.healthhub.sg/api/public/content/7551e2a78284495f929215d2ccd5456b]]

The rule covers bolsters too. Many Singapore families give children a small bolster to hug, but a bolster is a soft object like any pillow. Keep it out of a baby's cot for at least the first 12 months.

## When can a toddler start using a pillow?

There is no single official age after the first birthday. Some children's hospitals advise waiting until about age two, then starting with a small, firm pillow. [[cite: children's hospital guidance: do not give a child a pillow before age 2; after 2, start with a small, firm pillow; hold off if the child moves to a bed before 2 | https://health.choc.org/when-should-i-move-my-toddler-into-a-big-bed/]] Australia's Red Nose suggests waiting until your child sleeps in their own bed. One reason: older babies can use a pillow to climb out of a cot. [[cite: Red Nose: safer to wait until the child starts sleeping in their own bed before introducing pillows; older babies can use pillows or bumpers to climb out of the cot | https://rednose.org.au/question/at-what-age-can-i-introduce-cot-bumpers-and-pillows]]

Many toddlers sleep comfortably without a pillow, so there is no rush. If you are unsure, ask your paediatrician.

| Age | Pillow? | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 12 months | No | Back to sleep on a firm, flat cot mattress, with nothing else in the cot |
| 12 months to about 2 years | Usually not needed | Ask your paediatrician, and consider waiting until your child is in a bed |
| About 2 years and older, in a bed | Optional | Start with a small, thin, fairly firm pillow |
| School age | If your child wants one | Match the height to shoulder width and sleep position, and recheck as they grow |

## How high and firm should a child's pillow be?

Low and fairly firm: high enough to keep the head level with the spine, and firm enough not to fold around the face. We have not found paediatric guidance that sets pillow heights for children, so use the same alignment check as for adults.

For adults, a Cleveland Clinic chiropractor names a pillow that bends the neck forward or to one side as the most common mistake. [[cite: most common mistake is a pillow that bends the neck forward or to one side; too low points the jaw to the ceiling, too high points it to the chest | https://health.clevelandclinic.org/is-your-pillow-hurting-your-neck-7-tips-for-better-sleep]] The same check works on a child:

- **Back sleepers:** the chin should be neither tipped up nor tucked down.
- **Side sleepers:** the head should sit level, with the nose in line with the middle of the chest.
- **Too soft:** if the head sinks deep into the pillow, choose a firmer one.
- **Too big:** an adult pillow is usually too high for a small child. A small, thin pillow fits better.

A child who keeps sliding off the pillow or pushing it away may simply find it too high. Try a lower one before you assume they need more support.

## Does an adjustable pillow help kids sleep better?

An adjustable pillow can fit for longer as your child grows, but we know of no evidence that it improves children's sleep itself. Its real advantage is practical: you can add height without buying a new pillow.

That matters for side sleepers. As a child's shoulders broaden, the gap between shoulder and head grows, so the pillow needs more height. The same principle sets the height of an Mpillow, which is sized from shoulder length.

What helps children sleep more is enough sleep and a steady routine. These are the amounts recommended by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM), with the AAP's endorsement:

| Age | Recommended sleep in 24 hours |
| --- | --- |
| 4 to 12 months | 12 to 16 hours, including naps |
| 1 to 2 years | 11 to 14 hours, including naps |
| 3 to 5 years | 10 to 13 hours, including naps |
| 6 to 12 years | 9 to 12 hours |
| 13 to 18 years | 8 to 10 hours |

The ranges above are for healthy children and count naps where they apply. [[cite: AASM 2016 paediatric consensus, endorsed by the AAP: hours of sleep per 24 hours by age as in the table (4 to 12 months 12 to 16; 1 to 2 years 11 to 14; 3 to 5 years 10 to 13; 6 to 12 years 9 to 12; 13 to 18 years 8 to 10) | https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/healthy-sleep-habits-how-many-hours-does-your-child-need.aspx]]

The AAP also suggests the same bedtime routine every night, and no screens for at least an hour before bed. [[cite: AAP Brush, Book, Bed: a consistent nightly routine and regular bedtimes; avoid screens for at least one hour before bed and keep devices out of the bedroom | https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Brush-Book-Bed.aspx]]

## When is a child's sleep problem not about the pillow?

When a child snores most nights, gasps, or seems to stop breathing in their sleep, talk to your doctor. A sleep study may be needed to check for obstructive sleep apnoea. [[cite: AAP: talk with your child's doctor if your child snores; a sleep study may be needed to check for a sleep disorder such as obstructive sleep apnoea | https://healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/ear-nose-throat/Pages/Does-Your-Child-Snore-Video.aspx]] A new pillow is not the answer here.

Also see a doctor or physiotherapist if your child often wakes with neck pain or has pain after a fall. The same goes for numbness or tingling in the arms.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a 1-year-old sleep with a pillow?

The AAP's no-pillow advice covers the first 12 months, and most children do not need one straight after. Some children's hospitals suggest waiting until about two, or until your child moves to a bed, so ask your paediatrician.

### What kind of pillow suits a toddler?

A small, thin, fairly firm pillow, not an adult one. It should keep the head level with the spine and should not fold around the face.

### Should a child's pillow be soft or firm?

Firm enough to hold the head level without sinking, and soft enough to be comfortable. If your child pushes the pillow away or sleeps off it, it may be too high.

### Is the Mpillow suitable for children?

Mpillow has not published guidance on children using the Mpillow and does not describe it as a children's pillow. The Mbolster in size S, for children or people 1.50 m and below, is the Mpillow product described as suitable for children.

### How often should a child's pillow be replaced?

Check it the way you would an adult's: lumps, flat spots, smells, and whether it still fits as your child grows. Our page on [when to replace a pillow](/blog/how-often-should-you-replace-your-pillow-and-why-it-matters-for-neck-pain-amp-sleep-quality) lists the signs.

### Can a pillow stop my child snoring?

No. Regular snoring, gasping or pauses in breathing need a doctor's check, not a new pillow.
